# Credit Freeze and Fraud Alerts

> DO NOT PUT PLAINTEXT SECRETS HERE. Do not record Social Security numbers, dates of birth, ID numbers, credit bureau PINs, account numbers, passwords, or recovery codes in this file. Actual sensitive details belong only inside encrypted vault files.

Use this when identity documents, wallet contents, mail, phone access, tax records, or financial accounts may have been exposed.

## Choose the Right Action

- Fraud alert: tells creditors to verify identity before opening new credit. Useful after suspected exposure.
- Credit freeze: restricts access to credit reports. Stronger protection, but you must lift it when applying for credit.
- FTC IdentityTheft.gov report: use when identity theft has occurred or fraudulent accounts/transactions appear.

## Major Credit Bureaus

Contact each bureau through official websites or phone numbers:

- Equifax.
- Experian.
- TransUnion.

Keep freeze status, date, and non-secret confirmation notes in `templates/document-replacement-tracker.csv` or another tracker. Do not store bureau PINs or full identity data as plaintext.

## Other Places to Check

- Banks and credit card issuers.
- Payment apps and digital wallets.
- Phone carrier/SIM provider for port-out protection.
- IRS/state tax account if tax identity theft is possible.
- Medical insurance provider if insurance card or medical ID was exposed.
- ChexSystems or other specialty reporting agencies if bank-account fraud is suspected.

## Account Recovery Sections

- Google, Apple, Microsoft, and primary email: verify recovery phone/email and recent security events.
- Banks/cards: confirm freezes, replacements, and fraud investigations.
- Government IDs: replace lost IDs and report stolen documents through official channels.
- GitHub/OpenAI/cloud/dev accounts: review billing and account ownership if identity or payment methods were exposed.
